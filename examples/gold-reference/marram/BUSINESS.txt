Marram — a landscape and planting design studio on the Suffolk coast, based in Aldeburgh,
founded 2015, four people. It designs gardens for salt wind, thin soil and long dry summers,
planted so they need no watering after the second year, and works across Suffolk, Norfolk and
north Essex. Its position is that a garden needing a hose in August was planted wrong in March.

Its audience is private clients on the coast — often people who have already lost one or two
planting schemes to wind or drought — plus estates and small institutions with walled or
exposed gardens. The conversion goal is a paid first visit at £450, which includes a half-day
on site, a soil and exposure assessment and a written planting strategy, deducted in full if
the client goes on to commission a design. Selling that priced first step is what the site has
to do.

The studio does not use photographs of its gardens on the site; its visual language is a
register of the plants it specifies, shown as antique botanical plates.
