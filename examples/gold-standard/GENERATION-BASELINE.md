## The old score is not the comparison

Ardwell's recorded 8.711 from #255 is **OLD SCALE / NON-CONVERTIBLE**. It was produced on nine
criteria with no defined meaning for any point, five of the current thirteen were never asked,
and no pairwise comparison was made. Its honest position on the current instrument is 8.423
with a **LARGE** benchmark gap — which is a materially different statement from "passed at
8.711", and is why the re-score was run rather than the old number reused.

## What the jump consisted of

The two largest movements are the two things the old rubric could not see.

**`interaction-craft` 6.5 → 8.5.** Gen 1 has hover and focus behaviour; nobody had ever
photographed it. The criterion is scored on what the evidence shows, so a site with states and
no captures of them scores as a site without states. Gen 2 ships twelve captured states —
hover, focus, keyboard traversal, reduced-motion, held-touch, active navigation — and the
held-touch capture then found a real fixed-navigation collision that no page screenshot had
exposed.

**`responsive-recomposition` 7.5 → 9, and the ceiling.** Gen 1 triggered
`mobileIsStackedDesktop`, which caps the criterion at 7 whatever else is true. Gen 2 carries
authored crops from a three-ratio vocabulary and, more importantly, carries *weight* on the
phone: the 18,600 m² project bleeds past both margins, the 334 m² one is inset. The desktop's
opinion about which work matters survives at 390px.

**The gap moved LARGE → NONE**, and the reference is no longer stronger on any of the eight
pairwise dimensions. That is the result that matters more than the mean.

## The signature is the difference

Asked to name a moment belonging to this site and no other, the reviewer named for Gen 2:

> "The Work register draws six project frames and comparison bars in proportion to floor area,
> making the portfolio physically express the scale of the buildings being preserved."

And for Gen 1:

> "The alternating project index pairs monumental architectural photographs with terse proof
> statements — 'First submission', 'Less glass', '3 weeks early'."

Both are real devices. The difference is that Gen 1's is a *caption convention* applied to an
alternating grid, and Gen 2's is the composition itself: the layout is computed from the
business's own data, and no practice without a 55× range of project sizes could use it.
