--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PRODUCT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Interlock proves high-voltage switching schedules against a network model before anybody
opens a switch.

Before electrical work can start on a high-voltage network, somebody has to write a switching
schedule: a numbered sequence of operations — open this breaker, open that disconnector,
prove dead, apply these earths, issue the permit — that makes the work area safe without
cutting supply to customers who are not part of the job, and that can be reversed afterwards.

In the UK this is governed by the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989, and by each network
operator's own safety rules. Regulation 13 requires that equipment made dead is not
inadvertently re-energised while people are working on it. HSE guidance HSG85 sets out the
safe working practice the industry writes its rules against. The schedule is the document
that discharges that duty.

Today the schedule is written in Word, against a single-line diagram in Visio, checked by a
second Senior Authorised Person reading it line by line, and the customers-at-risk figure is
worked out in a spreadsheet. The three artefacts are not connected to each other. When the
network model changes, the schedules written against it do not.

Interlock connects them. It holds the network model, it simulates the schedule step by step,
and it refuses to produce a schedule that is unsafe.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT PROBLEM IT SOLVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A switching schedule can be wrong in ways that reading it does not reveal.

The dangerous errors are not typos. They are order errors and connectivity errors: an earth
applied to a point that is still connected to a live busbar through a disconnector nobody
opened; a permit issued naming points of isolation that do not actually isolate; a
restoration sequence that removes an earth before the permit is cancelled. Each of those
reads perfectly well on paper. Each requires you to hold the whole network in your head at
that exact step to catch.

That is what a second checker is for, and it is why the second checker is expensive, slow,
and human.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHO USES IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Senior Authorised Persons who write and check schedules, at:

  - distribution network operators (the 33kV and 11kV networks under the grid supply points);
  - large private HV networks — data centres, hospitals, ports, steelworks, universities —
    which run their own 11kV systems and hold their own safety rules.

Also outage planners, who need to know the customers-at-risk exposure of a proposed window
before they book it, and control engineers, who receive the schedule and issue the
instructions on the day.

Not electricians. Not facilities managers. The user holds a written authorisation to operate
HV apparatus, and has been assessed for it.

3. WHAT IT REPLACES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - the Word template the schedule is typed into;
  - the Visio single-line diagram it is written against, which is a picture rather than a
    model and therefore cannot be asked a question;
  - the second SAP's line-by-line check, which it does not remove but does change: the

  - the customers-at-risk spreadsheet;
  - the separate restoration schedule, typed out again by hand and reversed by eye.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE BEHAVIOUR THAT DIFFERENTIATES IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It refuses.

Interlock will not emit a schedule it cannot prove. When a step would leave the work area
connected to an energised part of the network, or would strand a customer the outage plan
said would stay on, or would apply an earth to a point that is not dead, it stops at that
step and says which earlier step is responsible.

The refusal names the cause, not the symptom. "Step 9 applies an earth to a point still
connected to Section 2" is useless on its own; "step 6 left T2-BD closed" is the sentence
that fixes it.

Everything else in the product follows from that. The step-by-step network state exists
because the refusal needs somewhere to point. The restoration sequence is generated because
the reverse of a proved sequence is provable and the reverse of a typed one is not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT IS IMPRESSIVE SHOWN RATHER THAN DESCRIBED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The schedule moving.

A switching schedule is a list of instructions, and a list of instructions is the least
interesting possible representation of what is actually happening: a network being taken
apart in a specific order, an island of dead equipment growing until it contains the work
area, and a number — how many customers would be off if one more thing failed right now —
rising and then falling back to zero.

Select a step and the diagram is at that step. That is the product. It is also the single
thing that cannot be conveyed by a paragraph, a feature card, or a screenshot.

The second thing is the refusal, because it is the only part of the product that argues with
its user, and because it is the reason to buy it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. OBJECTIONS THE SITE MUST ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Our network model is not good enough."
  It almost certainly is not, and that is addressed rather than denied. Interlock records
  what it was told, marks plant it has no model for, and refuses to prove a schedule that
  touches unmodelled plant rather than quietly assuming it is open.

"We are not permitting software to write safety documents."
  It does not sign anything. A Senior Authorised Person signs, as now. Interlock records who
  signed, against which model version, and what it proved at the time.

"We cannot connect anything to our control system."
  It does not connect to one. It works on the recorded network state and the planned
  operations. No telemetry, no control path, no route by which it could operate anything.

"How do we defend this to the regulator or in an investigation?"
  Every schedule is stored with the model version it was proved against and the full step
  state. A schedule proved eight months ago can be re-opened and shown in the network as it
  was understood that day.

"What happens when we disagree with it?"
  You override, in writing, with a reason, and the override is on the document. Interlock has
  no authority to stop a job. It has authority to make the disagreement explicit.

No invented adoption statistics. No customer logos. No "trusted by" wall. The evidence is the
product working on one job, in full.
