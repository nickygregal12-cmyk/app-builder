THE MONITORING GAP 2026 — prototype D, Gold Standard programme.

Published by Stell, a fictional independent freshwater data unit.

SYNTHETIC THROUGHOUT. Every catchment, every number and every figure in this report is
simulated. The catchments are invented and do not correspond to real ones. The regulatory
machinery is real and is described accurately, because a report that got it wrong would be
worthless to the people it is written for — but nothing here is a measurement of anything.

That distinction is stated on the report's own front page rather than in a footnote, because a
document that looks like this is exactly the kind of document that gets a chart screenshotted
and circulated without its provenance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SUBJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Storm overflows discharge untreated sewage into rivers when the sewer network is overwhelmed.
In England they are fitted with Event Duration Monitors — EDM — which record when a discharge
starts and stops. Operators report the counts annually.

The counts are the number everybody quotes. They are also, necessarily, counts of what the
monitors saw.

A monitor that is offline records nothing. It does not record a zero; it records an absence, and
an absence is not distinguishable from a quiet outfall once the figures are added up. Monitor
availability is published, and it is published separately from the spill counts, in a different
file, by a different route, and almost nobody joins the two.

This report joins them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE REPORT ARGUES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

That the spill figures cannot be read without their coverage, and that reading them without it
inverts the conclusion in the worst places.

Across twelve catchments over sixty months, mean monitor coverage was 70%. Reported spills per
overflow correlate with coverage at r = 0.66. The underlying rate does not: it correlates with
coverage at r = -0.19, which is to say not at all.

Published figures therefore track how much of the estate was working, not how much sewage went
into the river. The clearest single case is Elverley, which has the highest underlying rate in
the study and reports fewer spills per overflow than Cleeve Brook, which has half its rate and
twice its coverage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHO IT IS FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - environment reporters, who quote the annual counts and have no straightforward way to
    qualify them;
  - catchment partnerships and river trusts, who need to know whether their own numbers are
    comparable with the catchment next door;
  - regulators and the operators themselves, for whom this is not news but is rarely put on one
    page;
  - the people who live on these rivers, for whom "no spills reported" and "no spills" are two
    very different sentences.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE METHOD, AND WHY IT IS ON THE SITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dataset is generated, and the generator is published.

Each catchment is given a latent monthly spill rate — the discharges that actually occur, which
in the real world nobody observes directly. Monitor coverage is then simulated per month, and
the reported figure is what the working monitors would have seen. The relationship the report
describes therefore emerges from the simulation rather than being asserted by it: we did not
write the finding into the numbers, we wrote the mechanism and the finding fell out.

The first draft of the catchment table did not survive that claim. Every badly-monitored
catchment had also been given a high underlying rate, correlating the two at r = -0.87 — the
finding typed straight into the inputs. The check that caught it now runs on every build and its
output is published on the method page. A report that cannot pass its own test does not get to
make the argument.

That is worth saying plainly because it cuts both ways. It means the pattern is a genuine
consequence of the model. It also means the model is ours, and a reader is entitled to
disbelieve it. The generator, its seed and its parameters are all on the method page, and the
figures can be regenerated from them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE REPORT WILL NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - estimate unreported spills as a headline number. The report shows the coverage and lets the
    reader see the shape of what is missing; converting that into a single national figure would
    be the most quotable and least defensible thing in it;
  - rank the operators. The data does not support attribution and a league table would be read
    as though it did;
  - claim a trend. Sixty months is long enough to see coverage change and not long enough to
    separate that from monitors being installed at different times;
  - use a photograph of a pipe. Every report on this subject is illustrated with an outfall
    discharging into a river, and it tells the reader nothing they did not already feel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT IS IMPRESSIVE SHOWN RATHER THAN DESCRIBED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The coverage strip.

Sixty months across twelve catchments, drawn as a grid where each cell is one catchment-month
and the mark is coverage. Read left to right it is a history; read down it is a comparison; and
the pale patches — the months where half the monitors were dark — are immediately visible as
holes rather than as low numbers in a column.

Beside it, the same twelve catchments plotted as reported spills against coverage, with the four
that look cleanest circled. One is a picture of the data; the other is the argument. Neither
needs a sentence to be understood, which is the test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A report, with the apparatus of one: numbered findings that can be cited, numbered figures with
sources, a method section that is meant to be read, per-catchment pages so a reader can check
their own river, and a data page that gives the whole thing away.
