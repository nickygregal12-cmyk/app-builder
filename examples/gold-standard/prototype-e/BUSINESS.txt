HALLOWSAND
One house on a tidal island. Let by the week.

FICTIONAL. The island, the house, the owners and the estate are invented. The tidal
mechanism is real and computed; the photographs are real places, credited, and are not
Hallowsand.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT IS BEING SOLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A five-bedroom house on Hallowsand, a 60-hectare tidal island off the Northumberland
coast, reached by a mile of causeway that floods twice a day. The house sleeps nine. It
is let whole, by the week, Friday to Friday, at £3,400–£6,900 depending on season. There
is nothing else on the island but the house, a ruined chapel, a lime kiln and birds.

The proposition is not "a beautiful house by the sea". There are ten thousand of those
and they compete on photography, which is a competition this site cannot win and should
not enter.

The proposition is: FOR PART OF EVERY DAY YOU CANNOT LEAVE, AND NOBODY CAN ARRIVE.

That is the entire product. Everything else — the house, the beds, the stove, the view —
is ordinary and would be ordinary anywhere. The causeway is what you are paying for.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHO IT IS FOR, AND WHO IT IS NOT FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For: two or three families or a group of friends who want the week to be one thing
rather than a base for day trips. Writers and painters on a working retreat. People who
have been told to stop.

NOT for, and the site should say so in its own voice rather than in a caveats box:

  - anybody who needs to be somewhere at a fixed time. The crossing does not negotiate.
    Across the season the tide pushes one arrival to 20:00 and forces one departure onto
    a crossing that ends at 05:40. Both are computed, both are stated before payment,
    and we cannot move either.
  - anybody with a medical condition where a two-hour delay to an ambulance is not
    acceptable. The air ambulance can land; the road cannot always be used.
  - families with very young children who will find five hours of enforced stillness,
    twice a day, a punishment rather than a holiday.
  - anybody expecting deliveries, takeaways, a shop, or a mobile signal that works
    everywhere on the island.

A luxury letting site that omits this reads as marketing. Saying it is both honest and
— this is the commercial point, not a moral one — the strongest possible statement of
what the product is. The disadvantages ARE the product description.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE COMMERCIAL CONTRACT AROUND THE CAUSEWAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the part a generic holiday-let site would never have, and it is what makes
Hallowsand a business rather than a mood.

  ARRIVAL. Guests are given a crossing window for their arrival day, computed from the
  tide, not a check-in time. "From 15:40, and the road closes at 19:10" rather than
  "check in after 4pm". If a guest misses the window they wait on the mainland; there is
  a small yard at Cobb's Point where cars can stand.

  DEPARTURE. Same, in reverse, and it is the harder one. The last safe crossing on the
  Friday morning is sometimes 05:50. That is stated at the point of booking, per week,
  before payment — not in a document afterwards.

  THE WEEK YOU GET DEPENDS ON THE MOON, though by less than the first draft of this
  document claimed. Measured from the model rather than asserted:

    per crossing, at the extremes:  7h50 at neaps    6h57 at springs   (53 min)
    per crossing, week average:     7h34 neap week   7h00 spring week  (34 min)
    shut per day:                   9h45             10h08             (23 min)
    across a week:                  69h              71h44             (2h44)

  So the honest statement is: about half an hour more of road, twice a day, every day,
  in a neap week — and closer to an hour at the very bottom of the cycle. That is a real
  difference and it is not the gulf the marketing sentence wanted. Written before it was
  measured, this section said "fourteen and a half hours a day" against "shut for over
  ten"; the model says nothing of the kind, and the fix was to change the sentence.

  THIS IS PRICED, at 8% rather than the 12% first written, because pricing a difference
  larger than the one you are selling turns an honest trade into a gimmick. Spring weeks
  cost less in the same season band, because more people want the easier crossing. The
  site explains that rather than hiding a supplement, and expects a certain kind of guest
  to book the spring week deliberately.

  WHAT IS ACTUALLY LARGE is the drift: 50 minutes later every day, measured. No two days
  of a week have the crossing in the same place, and which hours you lose depends
  entirely on which Friday you started. That — not the spring/neap swing — is the fact
  the site leads with, because it is the one the model states most strongly.

  THE STORE. Because the road shuts, the house is provisioned rather than serviced. A
  standing order is placed before arrival and brought over on the last open crossing.
  This is not a hamper as an upsell; it is the logistics of the site working.

  WHAT HAPPENS IN A STORM. Surge is not in the tide table. A north-easterly can hold the
  water above the causeway through a window that the model says is open. The stated rule
  is that the estate's judgement overrides the table, and the table on this website
  overrides nothing at all.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE MODEL CAN HONESTLY PRODUCE  (src/lib/tide.ts)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two harmonic constituents, M2 and S2. What that legitimately gives us, and therefore
what the site is allowed to show:

  YES  - two crossings most days, about 5h20 of closure either side of each high water
  YES  - the ~50-minute daily drift. Measured at exactly 50 min/day over 58 high waters,
         and the strongest thing the model says
  YES  - the spring/neap swing, at the sizes tabulated in section 3 and not larger
  YES  - per-week totals: hours cut off, longest single closure, when it falls
  YES  - a comparison between any two weeks in the season
  YES  - how late the tide can push an arrival (20:00) and how early it can force a
         departure (last crossing ending 05:40). Both stated before payment

  NO   - real heights at a real place. The absolute numbers are invented.
  NO   - surge, wind, atmospheric pressure, or the state of the road surface.
  NO   - anything anybody should drive on. Said on the crossing page in the same size
         type as the table itself, not in a footer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ART DIRECTION — AND THE FIXTURE THIS MUST NOT BECOME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The benchmark reference file for this class carries its own warning:

    "Aman's language minus Aman's imagery is the 'minimal luxury slop' fixture."

That fixture exists in the calibration corpus as cc-21-luxury-slop.html: warm grey
ground, large serif display, enormous whitespace, a wide dim photograph, the words
"an escape from the everyday", and nothing in it that could only be said about one
place. If this prototype is built the obvious way it becomes its own test case, and the
critic that scored cc-21 at the bottom of its stratum would be right to do it again.

So the direction is taken from the product, not from the category.

  THE VISUAL MATERIAL IS THE TIDE, NOT THE HOUSE.
  A tidal island house photographs like every other coastal house. What no competitor
  has is a computed fortnight of the water. So the recurring graphic object across the
  whole site is the DAY RIBBON: a single horizontal bar, one per day, dark where the
  causeway is shut and open where it is passable, drawn from tide.ts. Stacked, a
  fortnight of them shows the drift as a diagonal you can see. It appears at four
  scales — a 24-hour strip in the header, a week in the booking table, a fortnight on
  the crossing page, and a whole season as a wall. The same object, four densities.
  This is the signature, and it is the thing that could not be lifted onto another site.

  GROUND. Not beige and not warm grey. A cold pale grey with green in it (haar, the
  sea fog off this coast), against a blue-black ink. The one signal colour is a wet
  ochre used for exactly one thing: WATER OVER THE ROAD. Shut. It appears in the
  ribbons, in the table, in the status line, and nowhere else — not on links, not on
  buttons, not on headings. A colour that means one thing throughout a site is a system;
  a colour used for emphasis is a decoration.

  TYPE. The tables are the product, so the numerals are the typography. A grotesque with
  true tabular lining figures for every time, height and duration, set at a size that
  says these are the point — the crossing table is set LARGER than the body prose, which
  inverts the usual hierarchy of a letting site and is the correct inversion here. Prose
  in a text serif at a proper measure, because the honest disadvantages section is real
  writing and needs to be read.

  PHOTOGRAPHY. Real, credited, few, and chosen from a contact sheet rather than from a
  filename (the lesson from prototype C, which put a ploughed field on a page about
  chalk). Subjects: a causeway under water, a refuge box, a lime kiln, the coast in
  haar. NOT: interiors, styled breakfasts, a couple on a beach, drone shots. Every
  photograph on this site shows either the crossing or the island's fabric. If a picture
  could be captioned "somewhere nice", it does not go on.

  MOTION. The header ribbon marks now against today's windows. It does not animate a
  tide. Nothing else moves.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Take every photograph off the site and it must still be obviously about one specific
place with one specific problem. Take the tide off it and there is no site left.

Conversely: if a paragraph of this website could be pasted onto a Cornish barn
conversion without anybody noticing, that paragraph is slop and comes out.
